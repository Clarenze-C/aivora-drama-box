# Thai micro-drama: research, season development and pilot decision plan

Research checked 19 October 2026. All money is USD unless marked S$. Prices exclude taxes unless specified. This is a production proposal, not a record of completed renders or audience tests.

**Cost correction after follow-up:** the original API-priced scenarios and unmeasured 5–40-minute local-shot sensitivities below did not evaluate FastH3, few-step turbo LoRAs or optimized attention workflows adequately. They must not be used as the expected cost of an optimized owner-operated pipeline. See [the optimized-compute addendum](optimized-compute-addendum.md) for verified implementations, conditional sub-dollar GPU costs and a revised benchmark. The original arithmetic is preserved for comparison; its high human-labor assumptions are also unvalidated.

**Recommendation: build and validate a two-gate automated pipeline.** The user has confirmed one approval for the season concept/arc, then final human review of each episode. All scripts, prompts, references, Thai audio checks, generation, editing, captions, AI pre-review, scheduling, publication and agency-style social operation are automated. Enforce **US$10 variable spend per episode up to five minutes**, including retries, with fixed subscriptions tracked separately. See [the controlling automation plan](automation-plan.md). An unattended, Thai-speaking, globally distributable production line remains a pilot hypothesis; no renders or integrations have been validated here.

Three issues change the proposed stack:

- H3 is real and supports 4–15 second audio-video generation, but Thai is absent from the vendor's 11 stably supported dialogue languages. A demo in another language does not establish Thai performance. [Official model repository](https://github.com/MiniMax-AI/MiniMax-H3).
- The community license's V.4 explicitly restricts displaying outputs outside its permitted territory, including the US, EU and UK. It also requires commercial UI attribution and additional authorization above $20m annual commercial revenue. Do not release community-weight output globally on the assumption that generating in Singapore solves this. Obtain written distribution rights or use a different licensed production route; API terms require their own review. [H3 license](https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3/blob/main/LICENSE).
- No official core node named **MiniMax H3 Voice Profile** was verified. Official reference-audio conditioning exists; it does not document a durable Thai speaker identity guarantee. A custom graph's label is not proof of such a capability. [ComfyUI H3 guide](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/video/minimax/minimax-h3), [official reference-node documentation](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/embedded-docs/blob/main/comfyui_embedded_docs/docs/MinimaxHailuo03ReferenceNode/en.md).

The public API terms describe generated-content ownership but no explicit worldwide H3 output-distribution override was found. Global API availability is not distribution clearance. Obtain written clarification covering the intended release territories before choosing that route. [API terms](https://platform.minimax.io/protocol/terms-of-service), [paid agreement](https://platform.minimax.io/protocol/paid-agreement).

**Scope assumption:** “Faceless” means no real performer needs to appear on camera; the fictional AI cast can have visible faces. If faces must literally never appear, use the same stories with hands, silhouettes, reflections and off-screen Thai performances, but recognition, expression and eventual UGC casting would need a separate test.

**Evidence notation.** **D** = documented by an official source; **I** = independent implementation/demonstration, not replication; **E** = explicit planning estimate; **A** = untested assumption or proposed creative choice. No render, private platform account, payout onboarding, publishing integration or live campaign was tested in this research. No costs were incurred for video production or advertising. This document supplies test specifications and decisions, not invented benchmark results.

**1. Audience opportunity and the format decision**

There is evidence that Thai stories travel, especially within Southeast Asia. Netflix reports that over 90% of its Thai members watched local content in 2025, and that 33 Thai titles had entered its global Top 10 by February 2026. That is evidence about Netflix's paying audience, not demand for an unknown AI micro-drama. [Netflix's 2026 Thai slate](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/next-on-netflix-thailand-2026).

For a closer emotional comparable, Netflix's distributor announcement reports record Thai-film box-office performance for *How to Make Millions Before Grandma Dies* in Singapore, Malaysia and other Southeast Asian markets. This supports testing family obligation and money conflict in the region. It does not establish English-subtitle conversion in the West. [Netflix release announcement](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/how-to-make-millions-before-grandma-dies-streams-september-12).

| Comparable or audience | Useful lesson | Boundary and proposed test |
|---|---|---|
| *How to Make Millions Before Grandma Dies* | Affection and self-interest can coexist; a practical act can carry emotion | Do not use terminal-grandparent inheritance competition, characters, scenes or marketing imagery |
| *Master of the House* | Status and family power can sustain serialized reversals; Netflix reports global Non-English No.1 | Premium live-action success does not validate cheap AI melodrama. Test earned betrayal rather than spectacle. [Netflix 2025 slate](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/next-on-netflix-thailand-2025) |
| Tao Kae Noi founder story, supplied by the brief | Ambition has an emotional price and business setbacks give concrete stakes | Treat as thematic reference only; no biography, seaweed-brand analogue or replication of entrepreneurial milestones |
| Thai domestic viewers, initially adults 25–44 (A) | Family work, care and dignity offer recognizably local stakes | Automated cultural/register checks plus audience response test local credibility; no routine editor gate |
| Singapore/Malaysia multilingual viewers and Thai diaspora (A) | Thai audio plus English subtitles may work across language communities | Segment Thai speakers from English-subtitle users; country does not identify language |
| Western Asian-drama fans and diaspora (A) | Existing tolerance for subtitles; family-business premise is accessible | Recruit separately from broad cold English-speaking viewers |
| Broad US, UK and Australian viewers (A) | A visible dilemma may communicate before cultural explanation | Test first-shot clarity, subtitle reading load and next-episode demand. Expand Europe country by country, not as one language market. |

The central barrier is not translation alone. Cold viewers need to understand who wants what, why it matters, and how to find the next installment. Unfamiliar honorifics, dense text, a slow opening, artificial acting and missing episode order can all prevent engagement. Keep Thai relationships legible through actions; translate meaning rather than removing Thai cultural specificity. Avoid poverty spectacle and a cartoon villain who only explains the plot.

**Localization package (A):** original Thai dialogue; automatically transcribed and independently checked Thai speech; English subtitles timed to the final audio; captions reviewed as part of the final human video gate; English title/description translations where supported; episode numbers and a stable series name; a single “start here” destination. Add Malay subtitles after evidence of Malaysian demand. Do not dub the pilot. On YouTube set spoken language to Thai and add separate English metadata and caption tracks; review or disable automatic dubbing if the account offers it so it does not silently replace Thai audio. YouTube documents translated metadata and timed subtitle uploads, but not guaranteed discovery gains. [Metadata translations](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6289575), [subtitle uploads](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2734796).

**Format recommendation:** 10 × approximately four minutes, released weekly after a two-episode buffer exists. Maintain a vertical dramatic master and an independently composed 16:9 full-episode version where practical. Do not crop essential two-person blocking from a horizontal image. Full episodes reward attachment; two 100–140-second parts lower initial commitment but introduce a navigation drop; 20–40-second promotional scenes test the premise. Make each part resolve a real beat. Parts are distribution edits, not a reason to stretch one revelation across repetitive posts.

Each garment carries an unresolved promise | Visually distinct and UGC potential, but wardrobe permutations increase identity/continuity load |

Choose *The Last Repair*, not because it guarantees reach, but because its character, conflict and production constraints fit a fair pilot. May can later host clearly fictional repair/product demonstrations. Keep commercial appearances outside canonical story conflict; label sponsorship and avoid fabricated personal product testimonials.

**Pilot logline:** A daughter tries to collect thirty unpaid repair bills before a rent deadline. Her mother's first supposed debtor arrives with proof that the family shop has already been sold.

Canon clarification: Noi's stroke occurred three months before episode 1; her recovery and work limitations remain visible and require informed Thai medical-context review. The relocation payment was accepted during that emergency; the ten-day clock begins when the family receives the repayment/clearance notice. Aunt Da formerly worked night-shift building maintenance. The receipt initially creates a mistaken impression of a sale, not legal proof of ownership transfer. The asset IDs below are proposed references to create, not existing generated reference images.

**Showrunner promise:** Each episode answers one practical question, changes one relationship and leaves one consequential choice open. No hidden twin, fabricated death scare, repeated “you won't believe this” or cliffhanger immediately explained away. May must make decisions, not merely receive clues.

**Four-minute episode shape:** 0:00–0:08 concrete contradiction; 0:08–0:35 establish the want and cost; 0:35–1:20 first attempted solution and complication; 1:20–2:10 a human detail changes the apparent motive; 2:10–3:10 a decision with a price; 3:10–3:45 partial payoff; 3:45–3:56 a consequence opens the next question; 3:56–4:00 legible next-episode path. Beats can breathe for 10–20 seconds when performance earns it; “development” need not mean a new twist every few seconds.

**3. Proposed ten-episode season (A)**

The ledger holds thirty unpaid jobs, but this season follows six representative objects and the family negotiations around them. Money is accounted for on screen. Use an internal ten-day story calendar; check any lease or medical references against reliable sources with the automated fact critic, flag uncertainty and avoid implying documentary accuracy.

|---|---|---|---|

| 8 — “No Charge” | Noi refuses to certify an untested unit even when rent depends on it | Aunt Da lends a calibrated replacement from her former workplace with permission; incomplete units are disclosed. Episode 8's workplace loan and calibration details are fictional planning choices for Thai technical review. No unexplained donation or viral windfall solves the finances. Maintain a ledger in the bible so revenue, refunds, deposit and savings reconcile.

**4. Repeatable original-story research system**

Run a weekly bounded research job, not an open-ended scraper. A researcher model can gather source candidates; Claude acts as story architect and returns structured proposals. Neither should declare an idea original just because search did not find it.

1. **Collect:** approved public creator channels, platform trend tools, distributor slates, licensed industry reports, news about everyday social tensions and consented viewer interviews. Use authorized APIs/exports for comments; do not bypass login, scrape private groups or copy full scripts. Sample 30–50 posts across at least five sources and code 100–200 comments across positive, neutral and negative reactions when access permits (E sampling plan).
2. **Record provenance:** URL, publication/event/retrieval date, market/language, source owner, observed claim, short permitted excerpt, rights/access method, duplicate/repost flags and whether figures are promotional. Separate “I would watch this” from demonstrated completion or payment. Do not infer a commenter's nationality from their language or username.
3. **Code emotions:** obligation, shame, debt, aspiration, resentment, repair, comic relief, reconciliation. Count comments at their source level; remove duplicates/bots where discernible; report sampling bias. Comments are qualitative clues, not market shares.
4. **Generate original combinations:** combine an emotional contradiction, a constrained recurring setting, a protagonist's active want, a cost of success and a repeatable object/action. Explicitly prohibit biographies, copied scene sequences, celebrity likenesses and recognizable persona imitation.
5. **Resemblance review:** search Thai and English working titles, loglines, unusual character traits and distinctive plot beats; compare against at least ten closest works and the supplied references. Review causal sequence and character constellation, not just wording. Flag overlap for rewriting; no numeric similarity threshold can guarantee clearance.
6. **Automated authenticity and fact review:** an independent language/culture critic checks kinship terms, pronouns, humor, class markers, geography, business practices and any health/legal claims against evidence. Record uncertainty; machine agreement does not prove cultural authenticity. Keep illness recovery realistic and avoid using disability as an easy villain cue. Optional audience research can test the result; no recurring human script-editor gate is required.
7. **Human commissioning gate — once per season:** approve the concept/arc, audience, character/voice design direction, production/rights route, US$10 episode cap and management policy. After approval, the system creates the detailed bible, scripts and prompts automatically. The only subsequent routine human gate is final episode review.
8. **After release:** ingest authorized analytics and anonymized feedback; log which predictions failed. Modify future beats without contradicting released canon. Do not rewrite every episode to chase daily trends.

Outputs: `research-ledger.csv`, `premise-cards.json`, `similarity-review.md`, `culture-notes.md`, `season-bible-vN.md`, `decision-log.md`. Each factual assertion carries a source; each creative inference is marked as such. Retain summaries and links rather than unnecessary personal comment histories.

Reference sheet: separate clean images per view/expression, plus one contact sheet for review. Do not ask the generator to render nine faces into a single image and assume it can read the sheet reliably. Store neutral lighting references, scene-lighting examples, full-body proportions and one approved two-person scale image. Generate scene starting frames from approved references, not previous degraded video alone. Add readable Thai receipts/signage in post.

Voice pack: automatically create/select recurring commercially eligible Thai voices after season approval, or use an existing consented recording library. Reuse locked voice IDs and references across episodes; no actor recording is a recurring production requirement. Maintain conversational, emotional and difficult-word anchors and extract legal-duration clips for each backend. H3's documented Ref2VA audio input allows up to three clips, each 2–15 seconds and at most 15 seconds total; do not send the whole library to a node that cannot accept it. Keep an approved 10-second neutral anchor and separate emotion anchors, with no music/reverb. [H3 input specifications](https://github.com/MiniMax-AI/MiniMax-H3).

**H3/Voice Profile assessment:** official Comfy workflows and reference-audio inputs are D; consistent Thai speaker identity across episodes is A. A third-party author explicitly describes experimental speech/voice behavior rather than a production voice-clone guarantee (I). Record its exact repository, commit, workflow JSON, actual node class and underlying API/model before using it. A graph that repackages reference audio is not a learned persistent speaker model. [Third-party speech validation report](https://github.com/T8mars/comfyui-minimax-h3-audio-T8/blob/main/docs/SPEECH_VALIDATION_REPORT.md).

**Recommended voice route:** automatically benchmark native H3 audio and a separate Thai-capable recurring licensed synthesis route. Existing consented recordings may supply calibration references, but hiring/recording an actor is not a mandatory episode step. Separately test a speech model with documented Thai support—e.g. Eleven v3, whose official language list includes Thai—and an approved licensed voice. Thai support does not prove tone correctness or stable acting. Free-plan commercial rights must not be assumed. [ElevenLabs models](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models), [commercial-use and cloning overview](https://elevenlabs.io/text-to-speech).

Obtain written scope for synthetic voice creation, dialogue, monetized episodes, territories, duration, data retention and deletion, compensation, provider uploads, and a separate opt-in for future brand/UGC work. Do not clone a celebrity, harvest an actor's movie dialogue or assume a platform stock voice is exclusive. Where a provider only permits self-verified professional cloning, have the speaker complete that provider's permitted process or use recordings; permission alone does not bypass product restrictions.

**6. Production feasibility and actual-render benchmark**

There is no render result in this plan. Before a season, compare three methods on the same 60-second scene with four 15-second generation units. Use three independent starts per method: 36 clips / nine minutes of candidate video before retries. Randomize viewing order and retain all failed takes.

| Method | Inputs and use | Likely failure to investigate | Decision rule (A) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: last-frame chain | Extract final decoded frame losslessly from accepted clip; use as next first frame | A still contains no velocity, breathing phase or audio state. Face/wardrobe/space errors become new truth; bad final frame propagates | Use for one continued action and stable camera, at most two linked continuations before a clean reference reset unless the benchmark justifies more |
| B: fresh shot keyframes | Approved scene frame + character and voice references for each shot | Pose/eyeline or lighting can jump; backend may not support all controls together | Default for shot/reverse-shot, new location, costume or time; keep a continuity manifest |
| C: hybrid edit | Fresh keyframes, selected continuations and reaction/insert cuts; approved audio spans cuts | Lip-sync route and sound coverage need editorial work | Prefer if it passes clarity at lower total accepted-minute cost, even if raw clips are shorter |

Important implementation distinction: H3's first/last-frame and omni-reference paths are different documented model variants. Do not assume a node can simultaneously lock the exact first frame, multiple identities and voice references. Inspect its actual input schema. If there is no supported combined control, compare available modes explicitly rather than promising a nonexistent connection. [Official ComfyUI workflows](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/video/minimax/minimax-h3).

Test separately: (i) unchanged costume/background, (ii) planned costume and location reset, (iii) moving hand passing a prop, (iv) camera angle cut, (v) two speakers, (vi) whispered anger and restrained grief. An intended costume change is not drift; compare against the per-shot intended state.

Score every clip at beginning/middle/end and every seam: face/silhouette, outfit, prop count, background geometry, motion direction, eyeline, lip alignment, exact Thai words, tone/pronunciation, speaker identity, emotion and ambient sound. At seams review 500ms either side at normal speed and slowly. Track chain depth and whether errors amplify from segment 1 to 4. Speaker embeddings and image similarity can flag changes but do not replace Thai listener review or catch all wrong-speaker errors.

**Voice screen:** three voices × four fixed Thai lines × three emotions × two independent generations = 72 utterances per candidate voice route. Include names, 300/30,000 baht, “ไม่” negation, kinship terms, question particles and a plausible Thai-English loanword. Three Thai listeners, blind speaker matching after hearing the reference; report confusions by speaker/emotion, not only an overall average. This is a screening set, not a statistical proof of season-long reliability.

**Acceptance targets (A, not industry benchmarks):** ≥90% blind speaker matching overall and ≥85% for each voice; ≥4/5 naturalness and emotional fit; zero wrong names, amounts, negations or speaker assignments in final selected lines. ≥95% intended words correct on first-pass usable takes, with every final line included in automated transcript/meaning checks. Measure Thai character/word errors with a declared tokenizer; tone/meaning assurance remains unproven without competent Thai assessment. The final user video review is the only routine human check. Final selection is performed by automated QA, followed by the final human video gate; no recurring native-specialist approval is assumed. Visually, no identity swap or plot-relevant continuity error in the approved episode; ≥90% selected shots rated ≥4/5 identity match. Check lip offsets around plosives/visible closures, aiming for ≤100ms on close-ups; this is an editorial target, not an assertion of H3 performance.

Fifteen seconds is a maximum storytelling option, not the unit of narrative thought. Generate 4–8-second shots and cut shorter if necessary. Avoid adding padding merely to consume a 15-second clip. Finish sound on a continuous timeline: choose one dialogue performance, preserve breaths, use room tone across cuts, and place music in the edit. Do not crossfade incompatible spoken words. Native clip audio may supply usable effects, but never stack multiple inconsistent music beds.

**Compute choice:** H3 official local ComfyUI support is documented, while a community 24GB-GPU setup is only a single implementation report (I). Benchmark exact checkpoint/quantization, resolution, frame count, GPU, system RAM, steps and peak memory. Some official preprocessing/2K components are hosted; local weights do not imply the entire official high-quality pipeline runs locally. [ComfyUI guide](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/video/minimax/minimax-h3), [community 3090 implementation](https://github.com/tonyd2wild/minimax-h3-local/blob/main/docs/3090-comfyui.md).

**Globally releasable fallback:** benchmark a separately licensed silent-video pipeline with consented Thai audio. Wan2.2's official repository licenses its models under Apache 2.0 and documents image-to-video plus a speech-to-video variant. That makes it a concrete rights-review candidate, not a claim that it matches H3 acting, Thai lip sync or 15-second throughput. Check the exact checkpoint, accessories, reference-image model and voice licenses separately. Audio-led storyboards can proceed using original artwork and recorded voices while the video route is chosen. [Wan2.2 official repository](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.2).

**14. Two-week automation pilot with continue, revise and stop criteria**

Use [the automation pilot sequence](automation-plan.md): one-time rights/account/infrastructure setup; optimized shot/voice and defect-detection benchmarks; fully automated script/reference/generation/edit/caption/QA stages; final approval; supported automatic publication and social operation; cost/reliability/audience review. No script, render, subtitle, edit or upload step is quietly assigned to a human to make the demonstration pass.

Gate 1 approves the season concept/arc once; Gate 2 reviews each final episode. The steady-state requirement is **US$10 variable costs per episode up to five minutes including retries and attributable management**. Fixed subscriptions and setup engineering are separately reported. Research/setup allocation and abandoned jobs remain visible. Exact model prices and benchmark coverage must be established before execution. A $10 cap is not evidence that adequate quality fits it. Paid advertising is not automatically funded.

Audience surveys/qualitative tests in the research plan are optional validation activities, not extra recurring production approvals. Automated Thai language/voice review remains a risk to measure. The final gate does not imply a fluent Thai specialist has reviewed the result.

**15. Ten questions only actual renders and audience data can answer**

1. Can three original Thai voices remain recognizable and correctly pronounced across restrained grief, anger and ordinary speech without speaker swaps?
