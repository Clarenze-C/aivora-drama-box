# Appendices

# Appendix J. Master Acceptance Checklist

This checklist is used at pilot greenlight, batch close-out, before release and at season close. Each item is marked
pass, fail, not applicable or unknown. Unknown requires an owner and a deadline; not applicable requires a stated basis.
A failed critical item can never be offset by a composite score.

## J.1 Commercial and audience

- [ ] The business model is explicit, and paid, ad-supported, branded or other routes do not mix the wrong metrics.
- [ ] The target audience is described by desire and viewing context, not only age and gender.
- [ ] The core promise has partial proof visible in episode one.
- [ ] Competitor research includes both successes and failures, with the original evidence preserved.
- [ ] Research observation, interpretation and decision stay separate.
- [ ] The pilot answers a specific unknown rather than "seeing how it goes."
- [ ] Budget, deadline, stop conditions and the decision maker are signed off.
- [ ] What acquisition creatives promise can be delivered within a reasonable viewing time.
- [ ] Commercial metrics include refunds, complaints or another trust guardrail.
- [ ] Scaling uses a reversible ladder.

## J.2 Story and season

- [ ] The protagonist's desire produces action.
- [ ] The antagonist has a sustainable method rather than just raising their voice.
- [ ] The series promise covers emotion, plot, relationship and visual.
- [ ] The season is derived backwards from its final choice.
- [ ] The A, B and C lines carry different functions.
- [ ] Releasing a secret changes a character's model rather than only adding information.
- [ ] Each act escalates strategy with visible consequences.
- [ ] The middle contains no pure stalling or repeated humiliation.
- [ ] The payoff ledger records what was borrowed, its progress and its delivery.
- [ ] The ending delivers both the external goal and the internal choice.

## J.3 Episode pack

- [ ] This episode's entry state matches the previous episode's exit.
- [ ] The opening question is specific and visible.
- [ ] The episode's change can be judged in one sentence.
- [ ] The episode repays at least one old promise.
- [ ] The new debt borrowed at the end is explicit.
- [ ] The next episode's first-shot obligation is locked.
- [ ] Every beat changes state, knowledge, power or debt.
- [ ] Every scene has an objective, strategy, escalation, turn and exit state.
- [ ] Fact, character knowledge and audience knowledge stay separate.
- [ ] Dialogue belongs to action rather than background explanation.
- [ ] Long narration is segmented by units of thought.
- [ ] Three timed reads fall inside the range.
- [ ] High-risk actions have insurance routes.
- [ ] Pack lint passes and several roles have approved it.

## J.4 Characters and assets

- [ ] Every core character has a stable ID.
- [ ] Identity anchors are observable features.
- [ ] Identity references cover frontal, profile, head-down, speaking, backlit and multi-person conditions.
- [ ] Age, fatigue, rain and injuries do not contaminate the identity layer.
- [ ] Characters are distinguishable by silhouette, hair, wardrobe and accessories.
- [ ] Wardrobe separates a base layer from overlay states.
- [ ] Injuries have a side, a degree, behavioral consequences and a recovery time.
- [ ] Locations have a floor plan, fixed landmarks and camera zones.
- [ ] Props have an owner, a holder, a position and a state.
- [ ] Key text assets are produced independently in post.
- [ ] Assets have provenance, checksums, rights, lifecycle and approval.
- [ ] Retired and quarantined assets cannot be selected by new tasks.

## J.5 Continuity

- [ ] Every shot's start state matches the previous shot's end state.
- [ ] Prop transfers are committed as events.
- [ ] Hands, injured sides, hair partings and jewelry are never mirrored.
- [ ] No character uses a fact they do not know.
- [ ] Relationship changes have evidence in action.
- [ ] The axis, screen direction and eyeline targets are explicit.
- [ ] Characters' world coordinates update as they move.
- [ ] Multi-person shots have slots and feature ownership.
- [ ] Light direction agrees with story time.
- [ ] Weather, wetness and environment state propagate by event.
- [ ] Music preserves theme, stems, tempo and bar position.
- [ ] Zero blockers in the state lint.

## J.6 Shots and generation

- [ ] Every shot has one primary narrative job.
- [ ] Shot requirements carry state, required visibility, prohibitions and acceptance.
- [ ] High-risk shots go to high-quality generation only after cheap validation.
- [ ] The static storyboard and the animatic with sound have been timed.
- [ ] References are chosen by angle and purpose rather than piled in indiscriminately.
- [ ] Prompts derive from structured facts and state.
- [ ] Unsupported capabilities block rather than being silently ignored.
- [ ] Each attempt changes only its controlled variable.
- [ ] Attempt logs contain hypothesis, result, cost and conclusion.
- [ ] Stop rules are enforced after repeated failures on the same invariant.
- [ ] Candidates stay quarantined after technical validation.
- [ ] Operator recommendation and final approval are separate.
- [ ] Approved takes have usable ranges and handles.
- [ ] Published material can be traced back to inputs, model and rights.

## J.7 Sound

- [ ] Core characters have a voice bible and anchors.
- [ ] The pronunciation lexicon covers names, companies, numbers and terminology.
- [ ] Line tasks carry the preceding and following lines, the target and the intent.
- [ ] Lip sync, body and voice are accepted separately.
- [ ] Voices stay stable across the units of a long narration.
- [ ] Narration and picture do not duplicate authority.
- [ ] Musical themes have narrative meaning and variant boundaries.
- [ ] The cue sheet records entries, exits, bars and stems.
- [ ] Ambience establishes one continuous space.
- [ ] Foley matches the real action state.
- [ ] Music ducks dynamically for dialogue.
- [ ] Phone speaker, headphones and low volume are all tested.
- [ ] Sound assets carry commercial rights.

## J.8 Editing and finishing

- [ ] V0 establishes causality first, without packaging hiding problems.
- [ ] The failed range of each selected take is excluded.
- [ ] A muted viewing conveys the main state changes.
- [ ] An audio-only listen follows the subject and the conflict.
- [ ] Reaction shots are used only when a judgment changes.
- [ ] Pauses serve thought and action rather than being distributed evenly.
- [ ] Transitions connect through causality, action or sound.
- [ ] Subtitles come from locked dialogue and break by meaning.
- [ ] Subtitles avoid faces, evidence and platform buttons.
- [ ] Phone, contract and chat graphics bind to facts.
- [ ] Grading normalizes technically before unifying style.
- [ ] Changes after picture lock go through impact analysis.
- [ ] Masters, clean versions, subtitles, stems and checksums are complete.
- [ ] Exports are re-imported and verified.

## J.9 QC and release

- [ ] Story, continuity, audiovisual, technical and rights are checked as five separate layers.
- [ ] Findings have a timecode, observation, expectation and evidence.
- [ ] Issues have severity, root cause, routing and regression scope.
- [ ] Duplicate findings from a common root cause are aggregated.
- [ ] Blockers are not offset by composite scores.
- [ ] Low-confidence model evaluations go to humans.
- [ ] Repairs happen at the earliest responsible layer.
- [ ] Closure has a fix version and regression evidence.
- [ ] The release candidate is immutable.
- [ ] The rights snapshot covers every published asset.
- [ ] Release files match the signed manifest's checksums.
- [ ] A second person independently verifies the release.
- [ ] Playback, sound, subtitles and events are verified at low volume.
- [ ] The rollback target and its trigger conditions are explicit.
- [ ] Platform receipts and remote content IDs are preserved.

## J.10 The agentic system

- [ ] Every agent has a responsibility, inputs, outputs and forbidden permissions.
- [ ] Tasks have an objective, context, budget, acceptance and an idempotency key.
- [ ] Capability tokens are short-lived and minimal.
- [ ] External content is untrusted by default.
- [ ] Context sections carry an authority level and provenance.
- [ ] Candidates, recommendations, validations and decisions are recorded separately.
- [ ] Model output cannot write canon or approval state directly.
- [ ] Duplicate events do not duplicate tasks, calls or settlements.
- [ ] Deterministic errors are never blindly retried.
- [ ] Budget exhaustion preserves evidence and stops.
- [ ] Vendor anomalies have a circuit breaker and a compatible fallback.
- [ ] Dead letters are fixed with new tasks, not by editing historical messages.
- [ ] The human queue has an SLO, priorities and backpressure.
- [ ] Prompts, adapters and evaluators can be staged and rolled back.
- [ ] Snapshot and event recovery have been rehearsed.
- [ ] Someone who did not build the system can take over from the runbook.

## J.11 Data, distribution and feedback

- [ ] Creatives are organized by promise family rather than as edit variants.
- [ ] Creatives show real proof and do not invent plot.
- [ ] The event dictionary is fixed before the experiment.
- [ ] Primary, guardrail and diagnostic metrics stay separate.
- [ ] Experiment variables and what is held constant are explicit.
- [ ] Early stopping rules are defined before anyone sees results.
- [ ] Clicks, comprehension, continuation, payment and refunds are analyzed together.
- [ ] Comments are deduplicated, clustered and bound to version and timecode.
- [ ] Comments become issues rather than editing the script directly.
- [ ] Fast and slow feedback are separated.
- [ ] Data-driven suggestions return through change requests.
- [ ] Failed experiments preserve the reason and their scope of applicability.

## J.12 Legal, contracts and organization

- [ ] The adaptation rights chain covers medium, territory, language and AI use.
- [ ] Real likeness and voice licences cover the specific scenes and derivatives.
- [ ] Music, fonts, stock and model terms carry versions.
- [ ] Rights records include term, platform and the revocation process.
- [ ] A rights revocation can list every derivative and release package.
- [ ] Client-supplied material has provenance and approval.
- [ ] Scope of work, revision rounds, acceptance and payment are explicit.
- [ ] Change requests show cost, schedule and rights impact.
- [ ] The boundary of source files and editable deliverables is explicit.
- [ ] Team roles, approvals and on-call responsibilities are explicit.
- [ ] Budget includes labor, failure, storage, rights and delivery.
- [ ] Cash flow and profit are managed separately.

## J.13 Scale

- [ ] Scaling proceeds by batch through a scale gate, never approving a whole season at once.
- [ ] Throughput is measured in approved seconds and approved episodes.
- [ ] Work in progress does not exceed the slowest gate's capacity.
- [ ] Cross-episode shared assets have their own freeze.
- [ ] Component versions are bound to production batches.
- [ ] Leads, key facts and the paywall boundary are 100 percent human-checked.
- [ ] Quality debt has an owner, a deadline and a stop threshold.
- [ ] Vendor switching and exit have been rehearsed.
- [ ] The system still runs when a key person is absent.
- [ ] Language and platform derivatives share one source of fact.
- [ ] Season close-out reclaims keys, processes deletions and verifies the archive.

## J.14 Absolute vetoes

Any one of these prohibits release: uncleared real likeness or voice; an error in a key amount, date or identity fact; a
lead visibly replaced; a prop end state that changes the plot; a character knowing a core secret too early; a release
manifest that disagrees with the files; an open security incident; no rollback capability; a published asset that cannot
be traced to its source.

## J.15 Sampling for a 100-item audit

A large project need not check every ordinary item by hand each time, but the absolute vetoes, lead close-ups, core
facts, the paywall interface and rights are always checked in full. The rest is sampled by asset risk, recent defects,
component changes and batch. A failed sample widens the scope; it is never averaged away.

## J.16 Project sign-off

```yaml
final_acceptance:
  project_id:
  release_id:
  checklist_version:
  passed_count:
  failed_count:
  unknown_count:
  not_applicable_with_reasons: []
  blockers: []
  approved_exceptions: []
  signatures:
    producer:
    story:
    visual_continuity:
    sound_edit:
    qc:
    rights:
    release:
```

Signing is not a transfer of responsibility. It confirms what evidence each person holds. An overall pass never overrides
an absolute veto.
